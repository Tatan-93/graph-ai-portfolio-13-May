  1. Daily Mosnodenvir as Dengue Prophylaxis in a Controlled Human Infection Model: Oral mosnodenvir significantly reduces DENV-3 RNA viral load in a human challenge model without serious safety concerns. Link
  2. Efficacy, public health impact and optimal use of the Takeda dengue vaccine: Mathematical modeling quantifies the potential population-level impact of the Qdenga vaccine across various transmission settings. Link

  4. Discrepancies in neglected tropical diseases burden estimates in China: Comparative analysis reveals significant gaps between Global Burden of Disease estimates and local real-world epidemiological surveillance data. Link

  7. A phase 1 trial of lipid-encapsulated mRNA encoding a monoclonal antibody with neutralizing activity against Chikungunya virus: A first-in-human trial assesses the safety and pharmacology of a novel mRNA-based passive immunization strategy. Link
  8. The 2021 report of the Lancet Countdown on health and climate change: Provides a baseline for evaluating the health-related indicators and indicators of climate-driven disease trends. Link
  9. Evaluation of the extended efficacy of the Dengvaxia vaccine against symptomatic and subclinical dengue infection: Examines the role of antibody titers as correlates of protection to address risks of severe disease outcomes. Link
  10. Estimating the health impact of vaccination against ten pathogens in 98 low-income and middle-income countries from 2000 to 2030: A large-scale modeling study projecting DALYs averted to support global health policy. Link

Relevance to my work

As a biologist and researcher with a background in data science and epidemiology, these publications are directly applicable to your work in the following ways:
  • Methodological Frameworks for Transmission Modeling: Several papers (e.g., #2, #3, #10) utilize sophisticated mathematical models to reconstruct transmission dynamics. These offer templates for how you might incorporate clinical trial data or seroprevalence studies into your own epidemiological models to simulate vaccine rollout scenarios or disease outcomes.
  • Data Integration and Validation: The study on neglected tropical diseases in China (#4) is highly relevant to your data science practice, as it underscores the necessity of reconciling global datasets (like GBD) with granular, local surveillance data. This highlights a critical need for your skills in data reconciliation and addressing bias in public health reporting.
  • Correlates of Protection: The research on Dengue (#1, #9) and mRNA-based passive immunity (#7) focuses on biomarker identification and the quantification of vaccine efficacy. This provides insight into how biological variables (RNA load, antibody titers) serve as proxies for epidemiological outcomes, which is foundational for developing predictive models in infectious disease research.
  • Climate-Sensitive Pathogen Forecasting: The Lancet Countdown reports (#5, #8) provide standardized indicators for the health impacts of climate change. You could potentially integrate these climate-related datasets into your existing epidemiological surveillance models to better anticipate shifts in the distribution or severity of arboviral diseases.
